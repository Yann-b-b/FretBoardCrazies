## A. Extended MAJOR colors — the avoid-4th is the whole story

- **The avoid note** is a scale tone a half-step above a chord tone; held, it destabilizes
  the chord. On a **major tonic the natural 4th (11) is *the* avoid note** — it's the
  unstable "characteristic pitch" of the key, and **tonic-function chords omit it**. *(Nettles
  & Graf, jazzguitar.be — confirmed 3-0.)*
- **Therefore the stable major tonic colors are maj9 and 6/9** (they add 9/6, never the 11),
  and **6 / 6/9 are preferred as final/ending chords — more stable than maj7** (no
  leading-tone bite). *(confirmed 3-0.)*
- **maj7♯11 (Lydian):** raising the 11 → ♯11 **removes the avoid note entirely** — Lydian has
  no avoid note. It's the scale for a **IVmaj7** (IV/Lydian function), but it *also* works as a
  **stable tonic destination** — an "ethereal rest," usable as a **I substitution at the end of
  a ii–V–I** rather than as a passing chord. *(confirmed 3-0.)*

**App rule:** per major quality, the color notes are {9, 6, ♯11(Lydian)} and the avoid note is
the natural 11 — a stable tonic wants 6/9 or maj7(♯11), never the plain 11.

## B. Extended MINOR colors — tonic family vs. the ii color (the key distinction)

The most app-useful finding: **not all minor colors are interchangeable — some are TONIC
colors and one is the ii color.**

- **Available tonic-minor chords:** the minor triad, **i6, i(maj7), and i7 (Dorian)** — the
  choice is stylistic. **m6's added 6th is always a *major* 6th.** In pre-1960s jazz, **m7 is a
  poor tonic minor** (it's heard as the **iim7** instead). *(Nettles & Graf, tamingthesax —
  confirmed 3-0.)*
- **m6 is the characteristic minor-*tonic* color** (tied to Dorian / melodic minor), **not a
  ii-function color** — emphasize the 6th only when the minor chord is the **i (tonic)**, never
  on a **ii** chord. *(UNVERIFIED — vote killed by spend limit, but standard.)*
- **m(maj7) = the tonic chord of melodic minor** (1-♭3-5-7 of melodic minor is its chord-scale);
  its major-7th creates **more tension than any other minor-family chord** and must be handled
  carefully. It's a tonic-minor color, fitting the **i of a minor ii–V–i**. *(UNVERIFIED.)*
- **The minor-line cliché** — the classic **i → i(maj7) → i7 → i6** descent, a chromatic inner
  line walking down from the root (root → 7 → ♭7 → 6). Famous as the *My Funny Valentine*
  opening (appears 3× — the "My Funny Valentine progression"); the descent can be routed into
  the **3rd of the following V7** (Dm–DmMaj7–Dm7–G9/B). *(UNVERIFIED — standard.)*

**App rule:** for a minor tonic, the color palette is {m6, m(maj7), m9, m6/9, m triad} and the
signature move is the m→m(maj7)→m7→m6 line; **m7 means "this is a ii," not "this is home."**

## C. Upper-structure triads over a dominant — the enumeration, resolved

An **upper-structure triad** is a plain major/minor triad voiced on top of the guide tones of
a more complex chord (a pianist/arranger device). *(Wikipedia — confirmed 3-0.)* The specific
spellings over **C7** (transpose by root):

| Upper triad | = C7 with | Tensions |
|---|---|---|
| **D major** (II) | C13♯11 | 9 / ♯11 / 13 — from **Lydian dominant**; best on **static/non-resolving** dominants |
| **E♭ major** (♭III) | C7♯9 | ♯9 |
| **G♭ major** (♭V) | C7♭9♯11 | ♭9 / ♯11 |
| **A♭ major** (♭VI) | C7♯9♭13 | ♯9 / ♭13 (alt) |
| **A major** (VI) | C13♭9 | ♭9 / 13 |
| **C minor** (i) | C7♯9 | ♯9 |
| **D♭ minor** (♭ii) | C7♭9♭13 | ♭9 / ♭13 |
| **E♭ minor** (♭iii) | C7♯9♯11 | ♯9 / ♯11 |
| **F♯ minor** (♯iv) | C7♭9♯11 | ♭9 / ♯11 |

*(Major set confirmed 2-1; minor set confirmed 3-0. **Refuted (1-2):** the meta-claim that the
"canonical set" is exactly 5 majors + 2 minors on degrees 2/♯2/♯4/♭6/6 — so the *specific
spellings above are reliable*, but don't treat any single "official list of N" as settled.)*

**App rule:** an altered/extended dominant color can be surfaced as "play an **X triad** over
the dominant" — e.g. *"13♯11 = a D triad over C7."* Great explanation material.

## What this adds to the app engine

Sharpens the earlier suggestion-graph + explanations with two concrete rules:
1. **Tonic-color stability ordering** (for "where a progression rests"): major tonic →
   **6/9 or maj7♯11 > maj7** (avoid the 11); minor tonic → **m6 / m(maj7) are home, m7 is the ii.**
2. **Upper-structure triads = the explanation layer for altered/extended dominants** (the table
   above): every color the app suggests over a dominant has a one-line "play *this triad* on
   top, it gives you *these* tensions" rationale.
