## 1. The master key: color = a mode of melodic minor

Most of the "hip" colors come from **four modes of the ascending melodic minor scale**, and
each one *is* the chord-scale that spells a quality's tensions:

| Chord quality | Chord-scale | Mode of melodic minor | Tensions it supplies |
|---|---|---|---|
| **7alt** (7♭9♯9♯11♭13) | Altered / super-Locrian | 7th mode | ♭9, ♯9, ♯11, ♭13 |
| **9♯11 / 13♯11** | Lydian dominant | 4th mode | 9, ♯11, 13 |
| **m7♭5** (half-dim) | Locrian ♮2 (or Locrian) | 6th mode (or 7th of major) | 9 available (♮2) |
| **sus♭9 / Phrygian** | Phrygian / 2nd mode mel. min. | — | suspended 4th + ♭9 |

*(Levine glossary via ejazzlines PDF; MTO review mtosmt.org; jazz-library — confirmed 3-0.)*
This is the unifying idea for the app: **a chord's "color notes" are just the non-guide-tone
notes of its chord-scale**, and which scale applies is a lookup by quality.

**Levine's "avoid-note" rule** tells you which scale notes are *dissonant* held against the
chord: the **4th over a major chord** and the **natural 11th over a dominant**. That's *why*
dominants take a **♯11** (Lydian dominant) not a natural 11, and why major tonics take **6/9
or maj7** rather than the 11. *(Levine — confirmed 3-0.)* → In the app this is the "avoid list"
per quality.

## 2. The families, by function

**Dominants (the workhorses of color).** A plain V7 upgrades along a spectrum: **V9 → V13**
(add the color extensions) or **V7♭9 / V7♯9 / V7♯5 / V7alt** (alter them). The altered tones
are **tendency tones** that resolve into the tonic; the guide tones (3rd/7th) still carry the
resolution. Its chord-scale is the altered scale (7th mode mel. min.). *(confirmed 3-0.)*

**Half-diminished (m7♭5).** A min7 with a ♭5; its home is the **ii of a minor ii–V–i**
(Dm7♭5 → G7♭9 → Cm). Scale: Locrian or the preferred **Locrian ♮2**. *(Levine, Open Music
Theory — confirmed 3-0.)*

**Diminished 7 (dim7) — the shape-shifter.** A **dom7♭9 with the root removed is a dim7**
(C7♭9 minus C = E°7). So every **dim7 is a rootless ♭9 dominant**, which is why dim7 works as
a passing/auxiliary chord and as a dominant substitute. Barry Harris's **dominant-diminished**
identity: every dim7 is the parent of **four dominants** (lower any one dim7 note a half-step
→ C°7 gives F7, D7, A♭7, B7), a mutually-substitutable ♭9 family; two dim7 a semitone apart
union to the **octatonic (half-whole) scale**. *(Barry Harris PDF, Levine — confirmed 3-0;
verifiable by pitch arithmetic.)*

**Sus.** A dominant 7 in which **the 4th is *not* an avoid note** — it stands in for the 3rd,
suspending resolution. **sus♭9** comes from Phrygian or the 2nd mode of melodic minor. Sus
**delays or replaces** the V→I resolution. *(Levine — confirmed 3-0.)*

**Tonic color: 6/9.** The **major-6/9** (and Barry Harris's **major-6 diminished scale**,
C-D-E-F-G-A♭-A-B) is the stable tonic color that sidesteps the maj7's leading-tone bite —
Harris's "borrowing" engine for comping lines. **minor-6/9** and **m(maj7)** are the parallel
tonic-minor colors (the minor-line-cliché lives here). *(Barry Harris PDF — confirmed 3-0.)*

*(Gap: m9, m11, m13, maj9, maj7♯11 were named but got little direct coverage beyond the above
— see open questions.)*

## 3. How they move (voice-leading & substitution mechanisms)

- **Tendency-tone resolution:** an altered dominant's ♭9/♯9/♭13 pull chromatically into the
  tonic while the guide tones do the 7th→3rd move — the color *intensifies* the same
  resolution, it doesn't change it.
- **Tritone sub (♭II7 for V7):** the two dominants a tritone apart share the same {3rd,7th}
  tritone (roles swapped), so ♭II7 resolves to I identically and gives **chromatic descending
  bass** (Dm7-**D♭7**-CM7). The **altered dominant and its tritone sub share melodic-minor
  material** (G altered = D♭ Lydian dominant), which links the two substitution paths.
  *(Open Music Theory, Wikipedia, jazz-library — confirmed 3-0.)*
- **Mode-mixture upgrades (the two most common):** **ii7 → iiø7** and **V7 → V7♭9** — both
  borrow ♭6 (le) from the parallel minor. This is the canonical way to "upgrade" a plain
  ii–V and to signal a minor ii–V. *(Open Music Theory — confirmed 3-0.)*
- **dim7 as connector:** a passing dim7 between diatonic degrees (e.g. I – ♯I°7 – ii) *is* a
  rootless ♭9 dominant pulling to the next chord.

## 4. How to deploy them (practical)

- **Upper-structure triads over a dominant** (a major triad on top of the guide tones): over
  C7, **A△ = 13♭9**, **D△ = 13♯11**, **A♭△ = 7♯9♯5 (alt)**. A compact way to grab a specific
  color as one familiar triad shape. *(Matt Olson PDF, Levine — confirmed 3-0.)*
  **⚠️ Caution:** a specific enumeration that the "important" dominant upper-structures are
  built on the **root/5th/7th (C/Gm/B♭ over C7) was REFUTED (0-3)**, and a claim that Levine's
  *Jazz Piano Book* Ch. 17 covers this was refuted — so the A/D/A♭ examples stand, but treat
  any authoritative *ranked list* of "which triads" as unsettled.
- **Rootless voicings (Bill Evans lineage):** four notes, **no root**, from 3/5/7/9(/13),
  alternating **Type A (3rd on bottom)** and **Type B (7th on bottom)** for smooth ii-V-I. A
  single rootless shape is **enharmonically reinterpretable across functions** — {A,C,E♭,G} is
  literally Am7♭5 and Cm6, and reads as a dominant/altered voicing over other roots — the
  economy that makes these colors learnable as a few reused shapes. *(Earl MacDonald/UConn,
  Levine — confirmed 3-0. Minor imprecision: the "F13" in that chain is strictly F9.)*

## 5. What this gives the app (the point)

The goal is an in-app engine that **suggests chords that go together, teaches as you go, and
explains why.** This research is exactly the material for it:

1. **A suggestion / "what goes with what" graph.** The confirmed relationships *are* the edges:
   - `V7 → {V9, V13, V7♭9, V7♯9, V7alt}` (upgrade/alter the same dominant)
   - `V7 ↔ ♭II7` (tritone sub)
   - `ii7 → iiø7`, `V7 → V7♭9` (minor-borrowing upgrades)
   - `dim7 ≈ rootless V7♭9` (passing/auxiliary connector; the 4-dominant family)
   - `I → I6/9` (tonic color swap)
   - minor ii–V–i: `iiø7 → V7♭9 → i(6/9 | mMaj7)`
   Each edge is a *musically justified* "these two sound good together," so the engine isn't
   guessing — it's applying functional harmony.
2. **Difficulty tiers for learn-as-you-go.** Order of unlock falls out of the theory: plain
   7ths → 9/13 → tritone sub & ♭9 → altered/upper-structures → dim7 & Barry-Harris borrowing.
3. **The explanations to surface while playing.** Every finding is a one-liner the app can
   show: *"a 13♯11 is a D triad over the dominant"*, *"this dim7 is really a rootless G7♭9"*,
   *"D♭7 subs for G7 — same 3rd & 7th, chromatic bass to C"*, *"iiø7 borrows the ♭6 from minor."*
4. **The grips come from your sheet, the logic comes from here.** The sources here are
   piano/theory-oriented (see open questions) — but you already have the **guitar voicings on
   your reference sheet**. So: sheet = the shapes, this research = how they connect and why.

## Caveats
Levine is the one true primary source; the rest are solid secondaries (peer-reviewed MTO &
Open Music Theory, reputable teaching PDFs) plus a single Barry Harris transcription PDF whose
claims independently check out by pitch arithmetic. **Two upper-structure claims were refuted**
(the root/5th/7th enumeration; the Jazz Piano Book Ch.17 attribution) — the *technique* is
well-supported, a specific "which triads" ranking is not. Minor enharmonic imprecisions noted
inline. Theory is timeless — no staleness risk.
