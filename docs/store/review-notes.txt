YOU DO NOT NEED A GUITAR TO TEST THIS APP

FretBoardMastery normally listens through the microphone to hear the notes you play. If you do not have an instrument to hand, the welcome screen offers a second way in:

1. Launch the app.
2. On the welcome screen, tap "I'll tap the fretboard" (the second button).
3. The drill now accepts taps directly on the on-screen fretboard. Tap the fret the prompt asks for.

You can switch between the two at any time in Settings > Game > Touch mode.

The app is landscape-only on iPhone and iPad. A fretboard is a wide object; portrait would shrink it below usability.

3. FUNCTION AND TARGET AUDIENCE
FretBoardMastery teaches guitar and bass players where every note sits on the fretboard.

The problem: most beginners learn shapes and tab without learning the notes underneath them, which blocks sight-reading, improvisation, and playing with other musicians. Memorising a chart does not transfer to the instrument.

The solution: a drill alternating two prompt types on a spaced-repetition schedule. About 75% of prompts name a note and string ("D — string 6") and you play or tap it; the other 25% highlight a fret and you name it. Wrong answers come back sooner. Progress is tracked per note-and-string pair and shown as accuracy, a streak, and a belt rank.

Audience: beginner and intermediate guitarists and bassists, self-taught players, and music students. No theory knowledge required.

4. SETUP AND ACCESS
No login, no account, no demo credentials or sample files. Every feature is available immediately at first launch.

Launch > choose an input mode on the welcome screen > the drill starts. Four tabs across the bottom: Drill, Progress, Tuner, Settings.

Settings holds three options: limit frets to the first twelve, enable the answer countdown, and touch mode (the same toggle as the welcome-screen choice). Instrument switches between guitar and bass.

5. EXTERNAL SERVICES AND TOOLS
None. The app makes no network requests of any kind — no URLSession, no networking framework, and no analytics, advertising, authentication, payment, or AI service anywhere in the binary. This is why the privacy declaration reports no data collection.

It links two MIT-licensed Swift packages, both running entirely on-device: AudioKit (5.6.5) for audio input and SoundpipeAudioKit (5.6.0) for pitch detection. Neither transmits anything.

Microphone audio is analysed in real time to detect pitch and is never recorded, stored, or transmitted. The prompt appears only if you choose "I'm playing an instrument". Declining it shows an explanatory notice with a button into system Settings rather than failing silently.

6. REGIONAL DIFFERENCES
None. The app functions identically in every region. There is no server, no remote configuration, no geo-gating, and no region-specific content. It ships in English only.

7. REGULATED INDUSTRY OR THIRD-PARTY MATERIAL
Neither applies. The app is not in a regulated industry and contains no third-party or licensed content — no recordings, songs, tablature, or artwork from any other source. All content is generated from music theory: note names and their positions on a standard-tuned fretboard, which are facts rather than protected material. The two libraries in item 5 are used under the MIT licence.

There are no accounts, no user-generated content, no in-app purchases, no subscriptions, and no advertising.
